511 S Eastern Blvd — 3.93-Acre Former Hotel Site — Broker Price Opinion

This report is for a prospective buyer of the former Ramada / Knights Inn site at 511 S Eastern Blvd, owned by AMG NC, LP. It covers what the land is worth, what to offer, and the risks to check before closing. The City of Fayetteville condemned the 1972 hotel in 2025 and had it demolished in 2026. The site is now free of buildings, but the building pad and underground utilities remain. It is 3.93 acres, zoned HI Heavy Industrial, with about 470 ft along US 301 (about 19,000 cars a day). The City’s demolition cost of about $280,000 is a lien on the land, and a former chrome-plating plant listed as an inactive hazardous site sits across the street. Both issues must be dealt with before you take title.

The report gives two values. As is, under today’s HI zoning, the land is worth about $275,000, or $1.60 per SF ($70,000 per acre). If the site is rezoned to CC Community Commercial, like the parcel next door at 525 S Eastern, closed sales of CC land support about $450,000, or $2.65 per SF ($115,000 per acre). The higher value applies only once the City approves the rezoning, so pay it only under a contract that depends on that approval. The as-is value assumes you take clear title, with the City demolition lien and 2026 taxes paid from the seller’s side at closing. For an as-is purchase, a suggested opening offer of $235,000 leaves room to negotiate. Do not pay more than about $300,000 unless the Phase I environmental report is clean and the old basement and pool check out. With a rezoning contingency, a price of about $375,000–$425,000 is reasonable. At the as-is value the seller owes about as much as the land is worth. At a rezoned price, the lien can be paid from the sale. This is a broker’s opinion of value to guide your offer, not a certified USPAP appraisal.

  • Get a Phase I before you close. To claim federal Superfund protection as a “bona fide prospective purchaser,” you must complete “all appropriate inquiries” before you buy. That means an ASTM E1527-21 Phase I, with key parts done within 180 days before closing (EPA; EPA liability guide). Your lender will also require it.
  • What the Phase I should cover. The Chrome-Rite file and which way groundwater flows; old heating-oil or fuel tanks from the hotel; what was used to fill the basement and pool; and any buried demolition debris. Ask the City for the contractor’s asbestos clearance and disposal records.
  • If it finds a problem. Expect a Phase II (soil and groundwater sampling), usually $8,000–$20,000 and several more weeks. Contamination that comes from Chrome-Rite and not from this site is usually that owner’s problem, but it can still limit groundwater use and slow financing.
  • State liability protection. If contamination is found, NC’s Brownfields program lets a buyer who did not cause it sign an agreement with DEQ. The agreement includes a covenant not to sue, passes to future owners, and gives a five-year property tax break on new improvements (NC DEQ Brownfields).

Zoning and likely uses

  • HI Heavy Industrial. The City’s development guide describes HI as a district for heavy manufacturing, assembly, fabrication, processing, distribution and storage (City development guide). The neighbor to the south at 525 S Eastern is zoned CC.
  • Neighborhood plan. The Central Campbellton Neighborhood Plan left the HI land east of Eastern Blvd out of the downtown rezoning. Heavy industrial uses remain allowed, and commercial and warehouse uses are permitted in HI. The plan notes the City “may need to address” the HI zoning on the east side in the future (Central Campbellton plan).
  • Cape Fear River Overlay. City GIS places the parcel in the Cape Fear River Overlay (CFRO) district, UDO section 30-3.H.10 (UDO overlay districts). Confirm any added design or use standards with City Planning.
  • Before you commit to a use. Ask City Planning to confirm in writing that your use is allowed in HI and the overlay, and what site-plan, buffer, landscaping and stormwater rules apply. A change of use can require bringing the whole site up to code.
  • Rezoning to CC. Two facts support a CC request. The parcel to the south at 525 S Eastern is already CC, and the Central Campbellton plan flags the east-side HI zoning for future City review. Approval is still not guaranteed. A rezoning needs a Planning Commission recommendation and a City Council vote, usually takes several months, and the Cape Fear River Overlay standards would still apply. CC would allow a wider range of retail, service and office uses; confirm the specific uses in the UDO use table.
  • A new hotel is unlikely. Hotel use is probably not allowed by right in HI, and rebuilding one would likely need a rezoning. Check the UDO use table.
  • Uses that fit. Contractor yard, truck or trailer parking, equipment storage, flex or small warehouse, or a service business that wants US 301 visibility. The paved pad and utilities are useful for yard users. A builder who needs a clean, compacted pad will have to deal with the old basement, pool and foundations.

Methodology

  • Approach. Sales comparison on price per SF of land, using closed sales of vacant industrial and commercial land in Fayetteville from 2023 to 2026, mostly within about a mile of the subject.
  • Sources. Prices come from county deed excise stamps ($2 per $1,000) and the county’s sales records. No comps came from the owner.
  • Adjustments. Each sale is adjusted for time (about 1.5% a year), size, usable area, site work and utilities, frontage and location, and terms of sale. The subject gets credit for its paving and utilities, offset by the cost and risk of the old basement, pool and slab. The percentages are the broker’s professional judgment.
  • Two values. The as-is value uses industrial land sales, since the site is zoned HI today. The rezoned value uses closed sales of CC and LC commercial land in Fayetteville from 2022 to 2024, adjusted the same way. It assumes CC zoning is in place, so it is a hypothetical value until the City approves. Active listings are shown for context only. Asking prices are not sales and do not set value.
  • Lien and environment. The value assumes you receive clear title, with the demolition lien paid by the seller at closing. The lien is the seller’s debt to settle and should not be added to your price. The neighborhood’s environmental history is reflected in the adjustments; a problem found on this site by a Phase I is not.

How the lien affects your purchase

  • The lien follows the land. Under NC law the demolition cost is a lien with the same priority as a special assessment (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 160D-1129). Your title insurer will list it as an exception unless it is paid at closing. Never close without a written City payoff letter and the lien paid from the settlement.
  • The seller is likely underwater at the as-is price. At $275,000, the lien (about $280,000), 2026 taxes ($13,583) and closing costs exceed the price. The seller would have to bring cash to closing or get the City to reduce the lien. That gives you price leverage, but also a real risk that the seller cannot close.
  • A rezoned price fixes that. At $375,000–$425,000, the lien and taxes can be paid from the sale with money left for the seller. That makes the seller more likely to cooperate with the rezoning application and to close.
  • Protect yourself in the contract. Make closing depend on the seller delivering title free of the demolition lien and all taxes. Give the seller a set period, such as 45–60 days, to get a City payoff or reduction in writing. Keep your deposit refundable until then.
  • A three-way closing may help. The City’s interest is getting its money back and the site back on the tax roll. Your offer can let the seller show the City that the full net proceeds go to the lien, which is often what makes a reduction possible.
  • Do not take over the lien in exchange for a lower price unless the City confirms the payoff amount in writing and your attorney approves. City fees and interest can grow while the deal is pending.

  • What the seller may point to. AMG paid $1,227,500 with a partner in 2021, and the 2026 buyout of the 39% partner at $690,944 implies about $1.77 million for the whole. Both reflect the hotel as a going business or the partners’ own accounting, not cleared land. The buyout deed was signed in 12/2025, before demolition, and is not an arm’s-length sale.
  • Older hotel sales. Operating hotels on 2–5 acres in Fayetteville sold for $875,000–$1,550,000 from 2022 to 2026 (for example 1922 Cedar Creek Rd, 3.0 ac, $875,000 in 8/2024). Those prices pay for rooms and income, which this site no longer has.
  • Higher commercial land sales. CC and LC land sold for $5.78–$7.47 per SF on stronger corridors or small in-town lots. Those sales are used in the rezoned value, with large downward adjustments for location and size. Suburban CC sites on Raeford, Yadkin and Ramsey Rd sold for $8–$28 per SF from 2022 to 2026; their demand is far stronger, and they are not used.
  • The next-door listing. The 5.70 acres to the south is listed at $1,250,000 ($5.03 per SF). It is an untested asking price on land that sold for $0.58 per SF in 2024.
  • Rezoning risk. If the City denies CC, the as-is value of about $275,000 applies. That is why the higher price should depend on approval.
  • Evidence for a lower price. C1, the land next door to the south, sold for $0.58 per SF in 2024. The county values the subject land at only $0.44 per SF.
  • Upside for you. The existing paving and utilities could save a yard user much of the cost of new paving. Both values give it only a small credit.
  • Downside. A Phase I that finds contamination, or a large cost to dig out the basement and pool, would justify an offer below the range or walking away.

Offer strategy & due diligence

Price

Path A, as is (HI): open at $235,000 (about $1.37 per SF), target $250,000–$275,000, and go up to about $300,000 only with a clean Phase I, sound soils at the old basement and pool, and confirmed utilities.

Path B, rezoning contingency: about $375,000–$425,000, closing within 30 days after the City approves CC zoning. The seller signs the rezoning application. If CC is denied, you can walk away or reprice to Path A.

Earnest money: about 1–2% of the price, refundable through due diligence and the lien-payoff period.

Key contract terms

Due diligence: 60–90 days, with access for environmental, survey and soil work. Lien payoff: seller delivers a written City payoff and pays the lien and all taxes at closing. Title: special or general warranty deed, with no exceptions for the demolition lien or taxes. Closing: 30 days after due diligence, or 15 days after the City payoff is set, whichever is later.

Due diligence checklist

  • City payoff letter for the demolition lien, including admin fees and interest, plus the contractor’s final invoice and asbestos clearance.
  • Title search, judgment search and an owner’s title policy. Confirm AMG NC, LP’s authority to sell.
  • ASTM E1527-21 Phase I, completed within 180 days before closing; Phase II if recommended.
  • Boundary and topographic survey showing frontage, the pad, the old basement and pool, paving and utility stubs.
  • Geotechnical borings where the basement and pool were filled.
  • PWC confirmation of the water, sewer and power services left at the site, and their size.
  • City Planning confirmation of your use in HI and the Cape Fear River Overlay, and NCDOT confirmation that existing driveways on US 301 can stay.
