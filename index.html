511 S Eastern Blvd — 3.93-Acre Former Hotel Site — Broker Price Opinion

This report values the former Ramada / Knights Inn site at 511 S Eastern Blvd, owned by AMG NC, LP. The City of Fayetteville condemned the 1972 hotel in 2025 and had it demolished in 2026. The site is now free of buildings, but the building pad and underground utilities remain. It is 3.93 acres, zoned HI Heavy Industrial, with about 470 ft along US 301 (about 19,000 cars a day). The City’s demolition cost of about $280,000 is a lien on the land, and a former chrome-plating plant listed as an inactive hazardous site sits across the street.

Zoning and likely uses

  • HI Heavy Industrial. The City’s development guide describes HI as a district for heavy manufacturing, assembly, fabrication, processing, distribution and storage (City development guide). The neighbor to the south at 525 S Eastern is zoned CC.
  • Neighborhood plan. The Central Campbellton Neighborhood Plan left the HI land east of Eastern Blvd out of the downtown rezoning. Heavy industrial uses remain allowed, and commercial and warehouse uses are permitted in HI. The plan notes the City “may need to address” the HI zoning on the east side in the future (Central Campbellton plan).
  • Cape Fear River Overlay. City GIS places the parcel in the Cape Fear River Overlay (CFRO) district, UDO section 30-3.H.10 (UDO overlay districts). Confirm any added design or use standards with City Planning.
  • A new hotel is unlikely. Hotel use is probably not allowed by right in HI, and rebuilding one would likely need a rezoning. Check the UDO use table. A new use or site plan will also trigger current landscaping, buffer and stormwater rules.
  • Likely buyers and uses. Contractor yard, truck or trailer parking, equipment storage, flex or small warehouse, or a service business that wants US 301 visibility. The paved pad and utilities are useful for yard users. A builder who needs a clean, compacted pad will have to deal with the old basement, pool and foundations.

Methodology

  • Approach. Sales comparison on price per SF of land, using closed sales of vacant industrial and commercial land in Fayetteville from 2023 to 2026, mostly within about a mile of the subject.
  • Sources. Prices come from county deed excise stamps ($2 per $1,000) and the county’s sales records. No comps came from the owner.
  • Adjustments. Each sale is adjusted for time (about 1.5% a year), size, usable area, site work and utilities, frontage and location, and terms of sale. The subject gets credit for its paving and utilities, offset by the cost and risk of the old basement, pool and slab. The percentages are the broker’s professional judgment.
  • Lien and environment. The value is the price a buyer would pay with the demolition lien paid at closing. The lien does not change what the land is worth, but it decides how much the seller keeps. The neighborhood’s environmental history is reflected in the location adjustment for comps outside it.

At the opinion of value, the lien and taxes use up the sale price, and the seller would have to bring about $37,000 to closing. At the full list price, the seller nets about $32,000. Interest on the 2026 taxes after 1/6/2027 and any City fees or interest on the lien would reduce these figures. The low end of the range applies if the Phase I finds a problem tied to the Chrome-Rite site, or the old basement and pool need costly excavation. The high end applies if a yard or truck-parking user values the existing paving and the Phase I is clean.

Options for the lien

  • Ask the City to reduce or settle the lien. The lien exceeds the likely net value of the land. The City has an interest in getting the site back on the tax roll and in use. A written request to Code Enforcement and the City Attorney, with this valuation, could support a reduction, a payoff from net sale proceeds, or a payment plan.
  • Walking away is not free. State law lets the City collect the demolition cost as a money judgment and attach it to the owner’s other city property, so leaving the land unsold does not end the debt (N.C. Gen. Stat. § 160D-1129).
  • Pay the 2026 taxes before 1/6/2027 to stop interest, and appeal the stormwater fee if hard surfaces change.
  • Correct the tax value. Ask the County to remove the demolished building from the 1/1/2027 assessment. That should bring the tax bill down sharply.

Counter-evidence

  • Owner’s own prices. AMG paid $1,227,500 with a partner in 2021, and the 2026 buyout of the 39% partner at $690,944 implies about $1.77 million for the whole. Both reflect the hotel as a going business or the partners’ own accounting, not the cleared land. The buyout deed was signed in 12/2025, before demolition, and is not an arm’s-length sale.
  • Older hotel sales. Operating hotels on 2–5 acres in Fayetteville sold for $875,000–$1,550,000 from 2022 to 2026 (for example 1922 Cedar Creek Rd, 3.0 ac, $875,000 in 8/2024). Those prices pay for rooms and income, which this site no longer has.
  • Higher commercial land sales. Frontage sites on stronger retail corridors sold for $5.78–$5.92 per SF (1885 Cedar Creek Rd, 5.16 ac, $1.3 million in 11/2022; 2028 Owen Dr, 2.81 ac, $725,000 in 5/2024). Smaller in-town sites near downtown sold for $7–$9 per SF. These are retail-zoned locations with far more demand and are not like this HI site.
  • Lower sales nearby. C1, the land next door to the south, sold for $0.58 per SF in 2024. The county values the subject land at only $0.44 per SF.
  • Upside. A user who needs a paved yard on US 301 now, or a future City rezoning of the east side of Eastern Blvd, could support a price above the range. Neither is assumed.
  • Downside. A Phase I that finds contamination from the Chrome-Rite site or old tanks, or a large cost to dig out the basement and pool, could push the price below the range.

Listing proposal & commission terms

Commission

6% co-brokered — of the gross sale price when a cooperating broker brings the buyer, split under standard commercial co-brokerage practice.

5% in-house — when Grant-Murray Real Estate, LLC brings the buyer directly.

At the $349,000 list price: about $20,940 co-brokered or $17,450 in-house. At the $275,000 opinion of value: about $16,500 or $13,750.

Price and term

List at $349,000 (about $2.04 per SF), leaving room to negotiate toward the $275,000 opinion. Suggested term: 12 months. Expect offers with a 60–90 day due-diligence period that includes a Phase I. The sale contract should say that the demolition lien and taxes are paid from the seller’s proceeds at closing, or as agreed with the City.

Before launch

  • Get the City’s written lien payoff, including fees and interest, and open talks on a reduction or settlement.
  • Order a title search and judgment search.
  • Order a Phase I ESA, or be ready to give buyers early access.
  • Order a boundary survey showing frontage, the pad, the old basement and pool areas, and utility stubs.
  • Ask PWC to confirm the water, sewer and power services left at the site.

Marketing plan

  • Offering package with the site map, survey, utility notes, zoning summary and the Phase I if available.
  • Direct outreach to contractors, trucking and equipment companies, outdoor-storage operators and nearby industrial owners.
  • Listing on LoopNet, Crexi and the local MLS, and a sign on US 301.
  • Monthly activity reports to the owner.
